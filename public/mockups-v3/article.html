The internet is still a place to read

A short argument for slower pages, smaller audiences, and leaving a little room for thought.

TRThraize Rowan · 6 min read · Today
For subscribers — This note is open today. Subscribe to keep reading.

We built the web around links, then filled it with rooms that ask us to stay. The best of those rooms still feel almost quiet: a title, a paragraph, a person who has chosen what is worth your time.

That is the promise of a publication. Not a stream that never ends, but a small signal that arrives when it has something to say.

Make the page smaller

Every extra control competes with the sentence underneath it. Remove the chrome and the work becomes visible again. Reading is an interaction too, and it deserves a surface built for attention.

So this is a modest proposal: let the feed be a front porch, let the article be a room, and let the people we trust decide what comes through the door.
